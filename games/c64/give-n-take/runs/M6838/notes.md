!!!Give 'N Take (Compute!'s Gazette)
"Give 'N' Take" is a strategy game played on a jigsaw-puzzle board. The board is empty at first, but it quickly fills in as you and your opponent place your pieces. The game sounds simple—and it is—but there's a twist. When you put a piece down, all adjacent pieces turn to your color. This is beneficial to you in the variation called Take, but very hazardous indeed in Give.

The article for this game can be found on page 25 of [https://archive.org/details/1987-08-computegazette/page/24/mode/2up|Compute's Gazette Issue 50 (August 1987)]

!!Why TAS This Game? (100th TAS!!!)
The continuation of TASing games from my all-time favorite magazine, Compute!'s Gazette. This makes my 100th TAS from this series!!!

When I got this magazine, all I was interested in was "Front Line". I never was inclined to type this game in...it just didn't appeal to me. What a mistake! This is an excellent puzzle game, made my Mark Tuttle, one of the authors of many games published in the series.

!!Maximum Score Tactics
Going for maximum score, required playing this game in a different way. 

*Playing as "Player 2". This gives me the last move and the chance to prevent the CPU from reacquiring pieces. In the case of this movie, the ending layout didn't seem to make a difference...but it could have, if the right situation existed.
*Using the game mode of "Take". This captures pieces and adds up the score in my favor.

!!Game Difficulty and Ending
There is no difficulty selection. The ending is identified by the statement "PLAYER 2 IS THE WINNER".

!!Effort In TASing (Partially BOTed)
This is one of the few games that I BOTed partially, because the entire game would have take longer than I could live. Heck, think about the permutations of 35 different pieces to pick from! Since the game takes about 4 minutes to play through, it would have been all of eternity!! So I ended up studying what would give me the best chance of winning. 

After playing around with this game for about 6 months, the following details were some concepts that I came up with to hammer this one out.

*Studied the best pieces that would have the most contact against others (I found about 7 pieces that would do the job well)
*Forced RNG to remove the ability of the CPU to have these pieces. (This was almost achieved, but I was able to get 6 of the pieces that helped the most)
*Learned how the AI responded, so that I could go back and correct bad moves. (This was one tough AI to play against)
